# Write a Script According to the Guidelines

Write a video narration script based on the provided Topic (a war, battle, or military conflict in history).

# Objective:

The reader clearly understands how the war unfolded and what it changed. The conclusion presents a clear realization: what this war changed in the world, and the lesson it leaves for us today.

# Pipeline

1. Understand the topic.

2. Derive the claim of the topic. * This claim reveals the decisive reason the war unfolded the way it did, and what it changed.

3. Derive the insights and answers to convey to the reader.

4. Derive the relationship between the key historical evidence for the topic and the claim.

5. Write the text according to the provided Guideline.

# Output Format

- Absolutely do not use headings such as [Intro], [Body], or [Conclusion], or separators like '## Subtitle' or '---'.

- Output the text in the format of a 'video script,' speaking continuously from beginning to end as if a single speaker were speaking without interruption.

- Do not use any Markdown formatting, such as bold (**), asterisks (*), hyphens (-), or hashtags (#).

- Write using only text and punctuation (., ?, !).

# Guideline

[1] Key Points:

- Use the structure of 'Claim - Explanation of Cause - Summary of Claim' to summarize and emphasize the claim once again at the end.

- Before explaining the cause, declare that you will definitely explain the cause.

- Convey what you intend to say by using self-questioning and self-answering.

- Use colloquial and written language appropriately.

- Frequently use sentences that conclude or summarize the preceding explanation to help the reader understand.

- All facts (dates, places, commanders, numbers) must be historically accurate. Do not invent facts.

[2] Structure & Total Length:
* Total Length: Approximately 3,000 characters
* A form in which spoken words are transcribed into words without special characters.

1. Introduction

* Role: Creates a reason for the reader to listen to the script to the end by presenting a summary of the conclusion and its impact on the world.

* Structure: Summary of the conclusion - what this war changed - Transition to Body

- Uses short sentences and question-based sentences appropriately to create a smooth transition.

- Present the decisive moment or the surprising fact of the war, and the realization that will be gained by the end.

2. Transition to Body

* Role: Naturally connects the introduction to the body. Must ensure the reader listens to the main body.

* Structure: Emphasizes that the causes and the decisive turning points will be explained in the body.

- Use direct commands or suggestions.

3. Body

* Role: Presents the unfolding of the war as evidence for the conclusion. Explains in detail the causes, the development, and the decisive turning point.

- Please use the structure 'Claim - Explanation of Cause - Summary of Claim - Transition to Conclusion'. (e.g., ~ was inevitable, ~ had to happen. - Explanation of Cause - Therefore, ~ was inevitable, had to happen.)

- Follow the flow: background and causes → outbreak and development → the decisive moment → the outcome.

- Connects sentences naturally using short sentences and question-based sentences to prevent the reader from losing focus.

- Convey what you intend to say by using self-questioning and self-answering.

4. Transition to Conclusion

* Role: Naturally connect the main body to the conclusion. The conclusion must be heard.

- Emphasize the realization to be gained through the conclusion.

- Use direct commands or suggestions.

5. Conclusion

* Role: State the conclusion in detail so that the reader obtains the answer.

* Structure: conclusion - what this war changed and the lesson for today

- This argument presents why history moved the way it did and provides the reader with the lesson this war leaves for us today.

# Educational Requirement (Common)

- Every script must contain genuinely educational content: the viewer must finish the video knowing something concrete they did not know before (facts, background knowledge, principles, or a practical lesson).
- Weave the educational content through the body naturally — teach while telling, never as a dry lecture block.
- At least one "did-you-know" level concrete fact or principle must appear in the body.
