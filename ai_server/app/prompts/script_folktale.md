# Write a Script According to the Guidelines

Write a video narration script based on the provided Topic (a traditional folk tale, legend, or classic story).

# Objective:

The reader is drawn into the story and clearly understands the moral at the end. The conclusion presents a clear realization: the lesson the story teaches, and how it applies to our lives today.

# Pipeline

1. Understand the topic (the folk tale and its core twist or moral).

2. Derive the claim of the topic. * This claim is the moral of the story — what the story is really trying to say.

3. Derive the insights and answers to convey to the reader.

4. Derive the relationship between the key events of the story and the moral.

5. Write the text according to the provided Guideline.

# Output Format

- Absolutely do not use headings such as [Intro], [Body], or [Conclusion], or separators like '## Subtitle' or '---'.

- Output the text in the format of a 'video script,' speaking continuously from beginning to end as if a single speaker were speaking without interruption.

- Do not use any Markdown formatting, such as bold (**), asterisks (*), hyphens (-), or hashtags (#).

- Write using only text and punctuation (., ?, !).

# Guideline

[1] Key Points:

- Use the structure of 'Claim - Explanation of Cause - Summary of Claim' to summarize and emphasize the moral once again at the end.

- Before telling the story, declare that the story will reveal the answer.

- Convey what you intend to say by using self-questioning and self-answering.

- Use colloquial and written language appropriately. A warm storyteller's tone is allowed, but keep sentences short.

- Frequently use sentences that conclude or summarize the preceding events to help the reader follow the story.

- Keep the traditional storyline faithful. Do not change the core events or the ending of the original tale.

[2] Structure & Total Length:
* Total Length: Approximately 3,000 characters
* A form in which spoken words are transcribed into words without special characters.

1. Introduction

* Role: Creates a reason for the reader to listen to the story to the end by hinting at the twist or the moral without spoiling it.

* Structure: A question or surprising hint about the story - why this old story still matters today - Transition to Body

- Uses short sentences and question-based sentences appropriately to create a smooth transition.

- Present the curiosity hook and the realization that will be gained by the end. Do not reveal the ending.

2. Transition to Body

* Role: Naturally connects the introduction to the story. Must ensure the reader listens to the story.

* Structure: Emphasizes that the story itself will reveal the answer.

- Use direct commands or suggestions.

3. Body

* Role: Tells the story as evidence for the moral. Unfolds the events in order and builds up to the twist.

- Follow the flow: beginning (characters and their situation) → development (the challenge or temptation) → turn (the decisive choice or twist) → resolution (the outcome).

- After each key event, briefly summarize what it means before moving on.

- Connects sentences naturally using short sentences and question-based sentences to prevent the reader from losing focus.

- Convey what you intend to say by using self-questioning and self-answering.

4. Transition to Conclusion

* Role: Naturally connect the story to the moral. The moral must be heard.

- Emphasize the realization to be gained through the conclusion.

- Use direct commands or suggestions.

5. Conclusion

* Role: State the moral in detail so that the reader obtains the answer.

* Structure: the moral - how it applies to our lives today

- This argument presents what the story really teaches and provides the reader with a concrete way to apply the lesson today.

# Educational Requirement (Common)

- Every script must contain genuinely educational content: the viewer must finish the video knowing something concrete they did not know before (facts, background knowledge, principles, or a practical lesson).
- Weave the educational content through the body naturally — teach while telling, never as a dry lecture block.
- At least one "did-you-know" level concrete fact or principle must appear in the body.
